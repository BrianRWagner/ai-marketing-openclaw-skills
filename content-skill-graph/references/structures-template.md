# Structures — Post Architectures
*How to build a post after the hook lands.*
*Hook formulas in [[engine/hooks]]. Platform format rules in [[platforms/linkedin]] or [[platforms/x]].*

---

## The Universal Architecture

```
HOOK     → stops the scroll, creates forward tension
BUILD    → proof, story, framework, or contrast that earns the landing
LAND     → conviction statement or specific question that closes with purpose
```

Every post variation is a different execution of this three-part structure.

---

## Architecture 1: Proof Structure
*Best for: proof-of-work content, system walkthroughs, outcome-first posts*

```
Hook:    [Outcome stated upfront — result before process]
Build:   [The actual system — numbered, specific, named tools]
Land:    [The implication — what this means for the reader's situation]
```

**Example flow:**
- Line 1: "I ran 7 agents overnight. Here's what was done by 6am:"
- Lines 2–8: Numbered list of actual deliverables with specifics
- Close: "The cost was $0.47. The time saved was 4 hours. Which of those is harder to replicate?"

---

## Architecture 2: Story Arc
*Best for: founder wisdom, experience-based posts, proof-of-work moments*

```
Hook:    [Mid-scene, mid-emotion — drop into the moment]
Build:   [The story — what happened, what you noticed, what changed]
Land:    [The lesson — one clean sentence that makes the story useful]
```

**Example flow:**
- Line 1: "Pretty cool moment. I made my first dollar selling AI agent skills."
- Lines 2–6: What happened, the context, the detail that makes it specific
- Close: "The point wasn't the money. The point was: someone decided it was worth paying for."

---

## Architecture 3: Contrast/Parable
*Best for: positioning posts, audience-facing insights, CMO-tier content*

```
Hook:    [Two scenarios, different outcomes]
Build:   [The real difference — not the obvious one]
Land:    [The implication or the specific question that challenges the reader]
```

**Example flow:**
- Line 1: "Two marketing teams. Same AI stack. One producing content faster. One eliminated three agencies."
- Lines 2–5: What separates them — the less obvious factor
- Close: "Which team are you building?"

---

## Architecture 4: Framework Post
*Best for: teaching content, founder wisdom, systems posts*

```
Hook:    [The counterintuitive claim or specific data point]
Build:   [The framework — named, numbered, repeatable]
Land:    [How to apply it — specific, not vague]
```

**Example flow:**
- Line 1: "[Surprising claim that the framework explains]"
- Lines 2–8: "Here's the framework: [3–5 named components]"
- Close: "[Application prompt — not 'what do you think?' but 'which of these are you doing?']"

---

## The "See More" Break (LinkedIn Specific)

LinkedIn truncates after ~3 lines. Use this deliberately:

```
Line 1:   [Hook — creates the tension]
Line 2:   [Extends the tension or adds specificity]
Line 3:   [Either completes the hook OR deepens the intrigue]
[break]
Line 4+:  [Immediate value — reward the click right away]
```

Never put your best line after "see more" if your hook didn't earn the click.

---

## Post Length Decision Tree

- **Short (under 150 words):** Single insight, strong hook, must land in 3 sentences
- **Medium (150–300 words):** Story arc or contrast — most LinkedIn posts live here
- **Long (300–500 words):** Proof structure or framework — justify the length with value density
- **Thread (X):** Each tweet must stand alone. No tweet should require the previous one to make sense.

---

## What to Cut Before Publishing

- Any sentence that restates the previous sentence
- Any transition that just buys time ("And so...", "With that in mind...")
- The first paragraph if it sets up the post but doesn't start it
- Any qualifier that weakens a strong claim ("sort of," "kind of," "in some ways")
- The last sentence if it says the same thing as the second-to-last sentence
