## Hook Formula 1: Outcome-First
State the result before the process. The result is the hook.

- `"[Real outcome you achieved. Here's how:]"`
- `"[Specific number + what happened. Total cost/time:]"`
- `"[Thing that was done while you weren't watching. Here's the system:]"`

**When to use:** Proof-of-work posts, system walkthroughs, results-driven content.

---

## Hook Formula 2: Contrarian/Counterintuitive
State the unpopular truth. Create productive friction.

- `"[Common belief in your industry]. [Why it's wrong or backwards.]"`
- `"Most [your audience] are [doing X wrong]. Here's what to do instead."`
- `"[The thing nobody admits about your topic]. [What you've learned.]"`

**When to use:** Opinion pieces, positioning content, taking a clear stand on something.

---

## Hook Formula 3: Specificity/Data
A surprising number that stops scrolling.

- `"[Surprising stat]. [Why it matters/what it means.]"`
- `"[Specific number] [outcome]. [Why that number is the story.]"`

**When to use:** Research-backed content, market insights, data-driven posts.

---

## Hook Formula 4: Story-Open (Mid-Scene)
Drop into a moment already in progress. No setup, no context.

- `"[Specific moment, mid-emotion or mid-action.]"`
- `"[Name] was [mid-scene detail]. [What happened next.]"`

**When to use:** Experience-based posts, founder stories, proof-of-work moments.

---

## Hook Formula 5: Question-as-Tension
A question that creates unresolved tension — NOT asking the reader a question.

- `"What does [thing] actually give you that [alternative] can't?"`
- `"Why does the same [tool/strategy] produce radically different outcomes for [two groups]?"`

**When to use:** Positioning content. Use sparingly — only when the question is genuinely interesting.

---

## Hook Formula 6: Parable Open (Two Scenarios)
Two parallel situations, different outcomes. Contrast is the hook.

- `"Two [groups]. Same [resource/tool/situation]. [One result]. [Different result]. The difference isn't [the obvious thing]."`
- `"Most [group] use [X] to [outcome 1]. A small group uses it to [different outcome 2]. The first group is [descriptor]. The second group is [different descriptor]."`

**When to use:** LinkedIn primarily. Executive audiences respond to pattern recognition.

## Hook Calibration by Platform

| Hook Type | LinkedIn | X |
|-----------|----------|---|
| Outcome-First | ✅ Opens thread | ✅ The whole tweet |
| Contrarian | ✅ With proof to follow | ✅ Let it stand alone |
| Specificity/Data | ✅ Lead with stat, build | ✅ Stat is the tweet |
| Story-Open | ✅ Full arc to follow | ⚠️ Only if one sentence completes |
| Question-as-Tension | ⚠️ Rare, only strong questions | ✅ Works standalone |
| Parable Open | ✅ Two scenarios work | ⚠️ Usually too long for tweet |

## Hooks to Avoid (Anti-Patterns)

- "[Context-setting opener that buries the lead]"
- "Here's something I've been thinking about..." → Momentum killer
- "I'm excited to share..." → Performative, not human
- "In today's [fast-paced/AI-driven/ever-changing] world..." → AI tell
- Starting with someone else's quote → You start with you
